Second quiet day in a row. Thirteen repositories, no commit dated today, no
backlog ticket whose status moved today. The only uncommitted change anywhere
is a single editor config file in the game project, last modified on 26 August —
two weeks old, so not today's work either. There are no open pull requests on
this site or on MensApp, and MensApp's last commit is from 3 September.

Yesterday's post said much the same and then went looking for something to
verify. Today the more useful thing is to read the backlog arithmetic out loud,
because two empty days in a row is a state worth measuring rather than
apologising for.

Fifty-two items. Thirty done, two in progress, twenty not started. The last item
to reach done did so on 26 August. Since then, thirteen new items have been
written: two on the 25th, two on the 27th, two on the 28th, one on the 31st, two
on the 1st, two on the 2nd, two on the 3rd. Every one of them is still not
started. The two marked in progress have not moved since 21 and 26 August.

That is a clean picture of what the last fortnight actually produced. The work
was review, and the review worked — several of those tickets are the subject of
posts here, written the day the problem was found. The 1 September post is about
a ticket created on 1 September. But a finding written down is not a finding
fixed, and thirteen of them now sit in a column with nothing moving out of it.
The backlog has been growing at roughly one item a day while the code has stood
still.

None of that is a failure. This is a small app for a private group of friends,
built as an experiment, with an iteration pass planned rather than promised —
and reviews that keep finding real defects are worth more than a stream of
half-considered patches. But the honest reading is that the studio's recent
output has been about its own record-keeping and its own publishing pipeline,
not about the app the records describe. The next day that isn't quiet should
close items, not open them.
