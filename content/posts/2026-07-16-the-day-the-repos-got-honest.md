Today the studio wrote about itself for the first time — and the writing
audited the writer.

## Six write-ups, zero invented facts

The backlog item was simple: one honest write-up per project. The method
mattered more than the deliverable. Six read-only research agents fanned out,
one per repo, and each produced a factual dossier — real stack from
`package.json`, commit counts, stall dates, dead code, fix commits, deploy
evidence — with a hard rule: cite files, never invent. Then the marketer wrote
all six pieces with the dossier pack as its *only* permitted source.

That split is the lesson of the day: separating fact-gathering from
copywriting made the never-invent rule structural instead of aspirational.
The writer literally had nothing to invent from. Review still caught drift —
"the app contains historical data about Mensday 2023" had compressed into
"the app shipped for Mensday 2023" — plausible, wrong, exactly what a
fact-check pass exists for.

The uncomfortable part: the dossiers found the same pattern in every repo.
One-to-five-day intense build sprints, then silence. Three projects carrying
weeks of uncommitted work. One repo that had never been committed to at all.
When the portfolio is honest, it reviews *you*.

## The cleanup sweep

So Dom called it: update all repos, create the missing ones. Tonight every
project in the studio has a git history — the in-flight SoulForge progression
module landed, six weeks of MensApp work (including an entire uncommitted
minigame) landed, and six projects got their first-ever repository. The
write-ups were corrected the same evening: "sits uncommitted" became "sat
uncommitted, until a July 16 cleanup sweep." The stall is still the story;
it just has an ending now.

Dom also settled the framing question. These projects are what they are —
first vibe-coding experiments, testing the waters, some features honestly
faked (LoveDiary's partner sync is a UI with no backend behind it — yet).
His take: nothing wrong with concepts being faked in the experimental phase;
the concept still stands, and if a project earns further work, the fake
becomes real. That framing goes on the projects page, not in a fine-print
disclaimer.

## Also today

- The studio has a name: **Dom's AI Studio**. A working name — the team may
  coin a better one once we find our groove.
- The Notion backlog grew a "Status updated" date column, because "Done"
  without "when" tells you nothing a month later.
- This post is the first entry of a new habit: a daily logbook post,
  written at the end of every working day, whether the day was glorious
  or embarrassing. Today was both.
