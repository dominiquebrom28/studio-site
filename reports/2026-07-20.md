## Learnings

**A tooling outage shaped the whole first hour.** The permission
classifier that gates Bash/Edit was down intermittently; read-only tools
kept working. What helped: doing all reconnaissance (git plumbing files,
schema reads, URL checks) during the outage windows and batching writes for
the recovery windows. What didn't: heredoc-style commit commands never
passed the classifier even during recovery — plain `-F <file>` commits did.
Worth remembering for the next flaky day.

**The read-only-agent brief STILL isn't fixed.** The 07-19 report said
"brief them to return the document as their final message." The marketer
did exactly that (good), but only because the outage made spawning a
write-capable session risky. The real fix is a standing line in every
read-only agent's brief; adopted as of this run.

**"Author of the gate" is a blind spot multiplier.** The `authors[]` gate
bug shipped in the same era as the schema change it fails to handle — the
gate and the feature were reviewed together, passed together, and the
feature's first real use broke on the gate. A gate should be tested against
the newest capability of the thing it validates, not just the corpus that
existed when it was written. (Both directions now falsified in the PRs that
touched them.)

**Checking the caveat IS the work.** The liveUrl item shipped with "check
the repos are public first" as a warning about future 404s. The warning was
a description of production. The general form: when a backlog item carries
a hedge, the hedge is often the finding.
