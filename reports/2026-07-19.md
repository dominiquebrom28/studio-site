## Learnings

**Test the test.** The smoke-test gate reported 7/7 green. I appended a dead
anchor to a blog post and it still reported 7/7 green — because it mounted
`getAllPosts()[0]` and nothing else, 1 of 11 content routes. The gate's own
justification is four runs of browser-only bugs; it would have caught the
07-18 anchor bug only because that bug happened to be *systemic*. A per-post
content error was invisible to it. **A gate that silently covers less than it
appears to is the same failure mode as a green build hiding a broken page** —
which is the exact thing that gate exists to prevent. Now 16/16 routes, no
sampling, with the coverage claim written into the file's header comment.

The general lesson: verifying a *gate* is not the same as running it. Running
it tells you it passes. Only breaking something on purpose tells you it can
fail. Both this and devops's own reintroduce-the-bug check were falsification,
and both found things.

**Two consecutive runs have now found a "verified" claim that was one layer
thinner than it read.** On 07-15 the Judge recomputed contrast ratios and
found two wrong. Today a green gate turned out to cover a ninth of its stated
surface. Neither was dishonesty — both were a plausible summary standing in
for the underlying check. That's worth naming as a house pattern, because the
mitigation is cheap and repeatable: *pick the load-bearing claim in any report
and independently re-derive it.* Four claims got re-derived today; one broke.

**A spec that names its own failure modes gets them built correctly.** Vera's
spec flagged the 44px tap target, the double-render risk, and the TOC-parity
risk in advance. All three came back right — and on the tap target Milo went
*further* than the spec, refusing to compose on `<Badge>` because a className
override after Badge's hardcoded padding depends on Tailwind class order,
which guarantees nothing. Compare 07-18, where the anchor bug had no such
warning and cost a P0. Naming the landmine appears to be most of the work.

**QA found the bug that only existed for content that doesn't exist yet.**
`poster` was optional for animations while the renderer falls back to `src` —
so a *future* project's GIF would have autoplayed under a "Play" button
claiming it hadn't started. Neither shipping file hit it. The best QA finding
this run was for content nobody has captured yet.

**Fixing an a11y bug found a product gap.** The play button unmounted on
click, dropping keyboard focus to `<body>`. The fix — a persistent Play⇄Stop
toggle — closed something nobody had written down: there was previously no way
to *stop* a GIF, on a feature whose entire premise is that motion should be
invited. The accessibility fix was also the better product.

**Read-only agents can't commit their own work.** Both designer and architect
returned excellent specs ending in "someone with write access needs to save
this." Their toolsets are read-only by design and that's correct — but I
should brief them to return the document as their final message rather than
plan a commit they cannot make. Small process fix for next run.
